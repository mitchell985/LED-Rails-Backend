# Map: Te Huia scheduled positions on the Auckland board

Label: wayfinder:map

> **Status: destination reached, 2026-09-18.** All 17 tickets resolved; the spec is at [spec.md](spec.md).

## Destination

A spec that an implementing session can build from, covering (a) a config-driven "scheduled train" pipeline that places Te Huia on the AKL LED board from static GTFS timetable data alone, and (b) a simulated-time setting in `/viewer` for testing it. The map is done when nothing is left to decide — no production code is written on the map itself.

## Notes

**Domain:** `LED-Rails-Backend` (Bun/TypeScript). Realtime GTFS vehicles → `updateTrackedTrains()` → `assignBlocksToTrains()` → `generateLedMap()` → versioned JSON the PCB consumes. See `CONTEXT.md` for the glossary.

**Skills every session should consult:** `grilling` + `domain-modeling` by default; `research` for research tickets; `prototype` for prototype tickets.

**Tracker deviation:** research findings are written to `.scratch/te-huia-scheduled-positions/research/<slug>.md` on the working branch rather than a throwaway `research/<name>` branch, because the working tree has uncommitted changes.

### Settled while charting (grilling, 2026-09-17)

These are standing constraints for every ticket, not open questions:

1. **Destination is a spec**, handed off — decisions only, no production code on the map.
2. **Representation:** a *synthetic vehicle* — position interpolated per update tick and injected as a `TrainInfo` into the live pipeline, so block assignment, display thresholds, `map.html` and `generateLedMap()` are reused unchanged.
3. **Generality:** config-driven (`scheduledTrains` in `config.json`), with Te Huia in AKL as the only configured instance. Not hardcoded, not a network-wide schedule fallback.
4. **Feed:** AT dev portal, *General Transit Feed V3 APIs - v3* (`https://dev-portal.at.govt.nz`). ~~Hand-encoded timetable is the fallback only if the feed doesn't carry Te Huia.~~ — superseded by ticket 14: the feed carries Te Huia, the fallback is dropped, and **AT's GTFS is authoritative for times**, with public holidays the one operator-rule override.
5. **Route through Auckland** (user-supplied, north→south): The Strand, Ōrākei, Meadowbank, Glen Innes, Panmure, Sylvia Park, Ōtāhuhu, Middlemore, Papatoetoe, Puhinui, Homai, Manurewa, Te Mahia, Takaanini, Papakura, Drury, Ngākōroa, Paerātā, Pukekohe — i.e. the Eastern line before joining the Southern — then off the network. **Stops only at The Strand, Puhinui and Pukekohe**; everything else is passed through. All of these blocks already exist in `railNetworks/AKL/trackBlocks.kml`.
6. **Geometry:** ~~an ordered block list in config → polyline through those blocks' centroids. No hand-drawn KML path, no dependence on GTFS `shapes.txt`.~~ — superseded by ticket 10: the **verified GTFS shape** supplies the path (the zip is pulled anyway), and the ordered block list survives as the *assignment* constraint rather than the geometry source.
7. **Interpolation:** distance-proportional along that polyline between the two bounding scheduled times.
8. **No realtime for Te Huia** — AT publishes none. Schedule is the only source; no realtime/schedule reconciliation is needed.
9. **Conflict rules** (only Te Huia ever yields; AT positions are ground truth and are never moved):
   - Same-direction train **ahead**: hold at least **one clear block** behind it, then resume toward schedule once clear.
   - Same-direction train **closing from behind**: run Te Huia **ahead of schedule** to stay a block in front.
   - Both rules are **suspended while Te Huia dwells** at The Strand, Puhinui or Pukekohe — it's platformed, so AT traffic passes.
10. **Identity:** its own route key and colour — **Te Huia brand yellow**. `TrackBlock.routes` is an allow-list parsed from the `[...]` suffix in KML placemark names, so the Te Huia route must be appended to `300 - The Strand [OUT-OF-SERVICE]` and to any other bracketed block on its path.
11. **Visibility window:** appears at its scheduled arrival into Pukekohe; at the northern end it appears 1 minute before departing The Strand. Off-board time shows nothing. **Amended by ticket 06:** visibility *starts* on the clock but *ends on arrival* — southbound it stays on the board until it reaches the end of the chain however late, and northbound it clears 1 minute after actually arriving at The Strand. Likewise a dwell requires the train to be in the station's block, not merely for the clock to be in the window.
12. **Simulated time:** `?time=<epoch seconds>` on the existing versioned endpoint (`/akl-ltm/110.json`), returning a **schedule-only** board for that instant (no realtime trains — reproducible by construction). Viewer gains date picker + service-day slider + play/pause + speed multiplier, falling back to live mode when off.

## Decisions so far

<!-- one line per closed ticket -->

- [01 — Does AT's GTFS v3 static API carry Te Huia, and how do we fetch it?](issues/01-at-gtfs-v3-te-huia.md): yes — `route_id HUIA-404`, `agency_id WRC`, 12 trips, `trip_id` = `255-<800005 NB|800006 SB>-<departure seconds>-1-<n>-<hash>`; v3 is a **JSON:API REST** surface (`/gtfs/v3/routes/{id}/trips` → `/trips/{id}/stoptimes`, ~31 KB for the whole timetable) with **no calendar/shapes/agency resource**, so service days need AT's separate unauthenticated flat zip `https://gtfs.at.govt.nz/gtfs.zip`; the existing `Ocp-Apim-Subscription-Key` already works on v3; **`downloadStaticGTFS()` cannot ingest v3 — a second JSON mode is required**; stops are The Strand `9241`, Puhinui `9216` NB / `9217` SB, Pukekohe `9233` NB / `9235` SB, with **`arrival_time == departure_time` everywhere (zero dwell in the feed)**. Detail: [research/at-gtfs-v3.md](research/at-gtfs-v3.md).
- [02 — Te Huia's published timetable and a reference for station dwell](issues/02-te-huia-timetable-and-dwell.md): Te Huia calls at **Pukekohe on every service** both directions (NB platform 2, SB platform 4; the WRC page's "Platform 3" is stale), and the GTFS shape **confirms map note 5** — it passes within ~100 m of every station on the user's list and stays 2,357 m clear of Newmarket, terminating at The Strand. **No published dwell figure exists** in any WRC / AT / KiwiRail / NZTA source, and AT encodes dwell elsewhere in the same file (~1.4% of rows) while encoding none here — so ~1 min is a chosen parameter, not a sourced fact. **WRC's published times and AT's GTFS times disagree by up to ~15 min.** Detail: [research/te-huia-timetable.md](research/te-huia-timetable.md).
- [14 — Which timetable is authoritative: WRC's published times or AT's GTFS?](issues/14-authoritative-timetable.md): **AT's GTFS wins** — it is 14 months fresher than WRC's booklet and the offsets are non-uniform, so the booklet is the stale artefact. Times *and* calendars follow the feed, **except public holidays**, where WRC's "does not run" rule overrides the feed's Christmas Day service (→ ticket 15). No WRC cross-check will be built (Imperva bot-check; not circumvented) — the feed is checked against itself instead: `feed_end_date` passed, trip count ≠ 12, `feed_version` changed. The hand-encoded timetable fallback is dropped.
- [12 — How is the Te Huia timetable ingested?](issues/12-ingestion-mode.md): **hybrid** — v3 API weekly for the 12 trips and 72 stop_times (~31 KB, existing `.env` key works); the keyless zip pulled *only* on a `feed_version` change (polled via `/gtfs/v3/versions`) for the calendar rows and 2 shapes v3 doesn't expose. Every fetch **distills to a Te Huia-only file** under `railNetworks/AKL` and discards the bulk, so runtime never touches the 127 MB `stop_times.txt` (the distiller must stream, not slurp). Weekly cadence; a failed fetch keeps the last good distillation. The existing `generateTimetable()` / MEL firmware path is **routed around**, not fixed.
- [04 — How do we decide a real train is "same direction" as Te Huia?](issues/04-direction-model-for-conflicts.md): **`direction_id`, on both sides** — a live feed sample found it on 9/9 in-service AKL trains, and Te Huia's own static trips carry it (`255-800005` = 0 NB, `255-800006` = 1 SB), so nothing is inferred. The per-route `direction_id` → chain-orientation mapping is **derived from the static feed at ingest** (amends ticket 12: distillation now also covers the 7 AT rail routes' directions). **Out-of-service trains are ignored entirely** — otherwise a unit stabled in `172 Wiri Depot Platform`, which sits on the chain, would hold Te Huia forever. **No limit on schedule drift** while held, but movement is **speed-capped** at track limits, or 110 km/h where none are found (→ ticket 16).
- [07 — The `?time=` endpoint contract](issues/07-simulated-time-endpoint-contract.md): `?time=<epoch seconds>` returns the board at that instant with **`timestamp` = the simulated moment** and an explicit **`simulated: true`** marker; **one request per instant** (no bulk day payload), so the viewer exercises the production code path; honoured on **any** instance with `Cache-Control: no-store` rather than a dev-only gate; also honoured on `/api/trackedtrains` and `/api/map`. Hard constraint: the simulated path must not mutate the shared `api.output` that real boards are served.
- [06 — Prototype: interpolation along the chain plus the hold/run-early clamp](issues/06-interpolation-and-hold-prototype.md): distance-proportional interpolation **holds up** over the long legs (no intermediate anchors needed) and the clamp is **stable, not oscillating**; the 110 km/h cap never binds on a clean run. But the prototype found a **defect in the agreed rules**: dwell and visibility were defined by the clock, so a held train **teleported ~22 km through the train blocking it** when its scheduled dwell came round. Both are now **positional** — dwelling requires being in the station's block, and visibility ends on arrival rather than on the timetable (**amends map note 11**). Prototype: [prototypes/te-huia-movement-PROTOTYPE.html](prototypes/te-huia-movement-PROTOTYPE.html).
- [13 — Where does dwell come from, given the feed encodes none?](issues/13-dwell-model.md): **60 seconds, chosen not sourced**, named as an assumption in config; the published time is the **arrival** and the dwell falls after it, so each onward leg starts a minute late and runs fractionally faster. **All three stops dwell**, and The Strand's visibility window is **derived from the terminus dwell** rather than being a separate display rule — which lands back on map note 11's original wording as a consequence. Deliberately not shared with the `DWELL_TIME_SECONDS = 30` in the MEL firmware path.
- [16 — Track speed limits along Te Huia's chain](issues/16-line-speed-limits.md): published sectional speeds **do** exist — KiwiRail's **LNI L1.1 Pukekohe – Waitākere** §1.20.5 — and Te Huia (class **Exp P**) is **100 km/h uniformly across all 62 blocks**, both directions. **This supersedes the 110 km/h fallback in ticket 04**: 110 is the AM-class EMU figure and covers only half the chain even for an EMU, while ETCS *pins* locomotive-hauled passenger consists at 100 rather than raising them. **The cap almost never binds** — all 48 timetabled legs average 36.3–61.4 km/h, and distance-proportional interpolation makes 61.4 the unperturbed ceiling, so it engages only if the run-early clamp exceeds 1.63× schedule pace. It is a safety rail, not part of the movement model. **The `DL — 80` caveat is closed** (owner, 2026-09-18): Te Huia is hauled by the **DLP** passenger variant, which runs at 100, agreeing with the Exp P class figure. Detail: [research/line-speeds.md](research/line-speeds.md).
- [10 — Fixing the 9 broken centroid-polyline segments](issues/10-path-geometry-fidelity.md): two fixes for three distinct faults. **Geometry = the verified GTFS shape** (free via ticket 12's zip pull), killing chord-cutting and the dark-LED class; **block assignment is chain-constrained** — a scheduled train resolves only against its own 62 blocks — which fixes the Papakura Sidings flicker and the northbound Quay Park terminus bug *by construction*, neither of which geometry could fix (the sidings polygon genuinely overlaps the running line). Outside every chain polygon, the **nearest chain block lights — never dark**. Realtime trains keep the existing geographic search. Leg distances must be re-derived from the shape (~51.9 km vs the polyline's 50.83 km).
- [11 — Does Te Huia participate in the altBlock mechanism?](issues/11-altblock-interaction.md): yes — **alt LED where one exists** (4 of 62 chain blocks, including Puhinui and Pukekohe where it dwells), hidden otherwise. The alphabetical route sort in `updateAltBlocks` is replaced by an **explicit three-tier priority**: real in-service > **scheduled** > out-of-service. So Te Huia yields to every real train but **outranks a stabled unit** — without which a unit parked in `172 Wiri Depot Platform` would blank Te Huia at the same spot every run. No behaviour change for existing networks. Collisions are routine, not rare: one polygon spans both tracks, so every opposite-direction train shares a block for a tick.
- [05 — The `scheduledTrains` config schema](issues/05-scheduled-trains-config-schema.md): a top-level `scheduledTrains` array (not nested under `GTFSStaticAPI`, which belongs to the MEL path). Identity is the feed's **`route_id` `HUIA-404` verbatim** — colours map, KML allow-list and LED payload all use it. **One chain north→south, reversed** for the other direction. The distilled timetable is **committed** under `railNetworks/AKL/` (~190 KB, dominated by the two shapes). Colour sits in the existing `LEDRailsAPI.colors` map — **append it**, since colour ids are positional — and **visibility is not configured at all**, being fully derived by tickets 06 and 13.
- [15 — Which public holidays suppress Te Huia, and where do the dates come from?](issues/15-public-holiday-suppression.md): **one committed `noServiceDates.json`** merging public holidays and planned track closures, each with a reason (so the 3-day Matariki closure needs no extra concept). Scope: the eleven national holidays **plus Auckland Anniversary**, and **both the actual and observed day** when Mondayised. A table rather than computed rules, because **Matariki is legislated, not computable**. The list **overrides the feed's `calendar_dates`**. If it runs out: **fail open** and warn loudly — suppressing every future date is worse than one phantom. Amends ticket 05's `suppressOnPublicHolidays` to `noServiceFile`.
- [08 — Prototype: the viewer's time control](issues/08-viewer-time-control-prototype.md): **variant A, the transport bar** — bottom bar, day-spanning scrubber as the primary affordance, play/pause, 1×/10×/60×, LIVE↔SIMULATED pill, whole bar amber while simulated. The viewer reads wall clock in **three places** (`viewer.html:442`, `:503`, `:517`), all of which misrender a simulated `timestamp`; they become a single **`nowSeconds()`** helper keyed off ticket 07's `simulated: true` marker (the prototype's global `Date.now()` override works but must not ship). Playback is rAF-driven, confirming the viewer must ignore `update` in simulated mode. Prototype: [prototypes/viewer-time-PROTOTYPE.html](prototypes/viewer-time-PROTOTYPE.html).
- [09 — Assemble the handoff spec](issues/09-assemble-handoff-spec.md): **[spec.md](spec.md)** — the destination. Resolved two fog items in passing: a held ghost emits `b: [X, X]` like any stationary train (the clamp needs no payload handling), and timezone/`24:00:00` handling folded into service-day resolution.
- [03 — Trace Te Huia's ordered block chain and the board's track/direction model](issues/03-board-topology-block-chain.md): the chain is 62 blocks, `300 → 322 → 324 … 341 → 159 → 160 → 162 … 205 → 207` (~50.9 km, all 19 stations; Quay Park uses only block 322, never the 320/321 triangle chords); `(+N)` is a spare LED for a *second train in the same block*, not a track or direction; AKL's KML carries **zero descriptions**, so there is no bearing, platform or track data in the block model at all; `300 - The Strand` is the only allow-listed block on the chain; and the centroid polyline breaks on 9 of 61 segments. Detail: [research/board-topology.md](research/board-topology.md).

## Not yet specified

- Whether AT's realtime feed really carries no Te Huia vehicle (map note 8). Ticket 01's check was inconclusive — sampled at 22:33 NZST, after the last service. Cheap to settle by sampling during service hours.
- Whether a backstop is needed for a Te Huia held indefinitely. Since ticket 06 made visibility end on arrival, a ghost blocked by a train that never moves stays lit forever — an in-service unit stabled on the chain could pin the LED on overnight. A grace period was declined; this is the residual.
- Whether `updateAltBlocks` assigning **both** the second and third train to the same `altBlock` (`i === 1 || i === 2`, `trackBlocks.ts:871`) is deliberate or an off-by-one. Reachable on this route when two real in-service trains and Te Huia share a block.
- Whether `map.html` should render the ghost distinctly from realtime vehicles.
- `TrainInfo.position.bearing` is declared `number | undefined`, but AT sends a **string** (`'264.7'`) when non-zero and `int 0` otherwise. Not used by this design (ticket 04 chose `direction_id`), so it is a latent bug in existing code rather than a decision on this route — but anything that starts comparing bearings will trip over it.
- Whether Te Huia should also feed the offline firmware timetable path (`HeaderGenerator`, `MEL_V1_0_0_Timetable.h`).

## Out of scope

- The hand-encoded timetable fallback (originally map note 4). Ticket 01 proved AT's feed carries Te Huia in full, so the contingency never arose; keeping it would mean a second timetable source free to drift from the feed. Ruled out in [ticket 14](issues/14-authoritative-timetable.md).
- Any automated cross-check of the feed against WRC's published timetable: their page is behind an Imperva bot-check and circumventing it is not on the table. Ruled out in [ticket 14](issues/14-authoritative-timetable.md).
- Stale route names in the AKL KML: blocks `139` and `151` still name `WEST-201` / `ONE-201` in their allow-lists while `config.json` has moved to `E-W-201` / `O-W-201` / `S-C-201`. Noticed by ticket 03, off Te Huia's chain, and a pre-existing bug rather than a step on this route.

- Realtime feed integration for Te Huia — AT publishes none (settled while charting, note 8).
- Schedule-derived ghost positions for AT trains generally (the "fully general schedule fallback" option was rejected while charting).
- Firmware / PCB changes: the board consumes the existing payload shape unchanged.
- Configuring scheduled services for WLG or MEL — the config schema must allow it, but no other network is set up in this effort.
